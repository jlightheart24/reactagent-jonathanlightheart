## Final result

- For a question about the current FDA model-code rule, the FDA Food Code 2026 controls: it is FDA's current model code, described as "FDA's best advice for a uniform system of provisions" for retail/foodservice, offered for adoption by local, state, and federal jurisdictions. [food-code-2026:p3:c0; food-code-2026:p8:c2]
- Even the 2026 Food Code is a model, not self-executing law: "The model Food Code is neither federal law nor federal regulation and is not preemptive," and it becomes enforceable only where a jurisdiction enacts it into statute, promulgates it as a regulation, or adopts it as an ordinance. [food-code-2026:p8:c2; food-code-2026:p13:c1; food-code-2026:p9:c0]
- The Employee Health and Personal Hygiene Handbook is FDA operational guidance that expressly states its information was updated to reflect the 2017 FDA Food Code and that it was "Adapted from the 2017 FDA Food Code"; it is therefore not the current model-code text and cannot override the 2026 edition. [employee-health-handbook:p3:c0; employee-health-handbook:p4:c2]
- Under the controlling 2026 text, vomiting is a reportable symptom (¶ 2-201.11(A)(1)(a)) that the food employee or conditional employee must report to the person in charge, and the person in charge must exclude a food employee who is symptomatic with vomiting (¶ 2-201.12(A)(1)) except when the symptom is from a noninfectious condition. [food-code-2026:p56:c1; food-code-2026:p58:c1; food-code-2026:p59:c0]
- Reinstatement under the 2026 Code: the person in charge may reinstate an employee excluded under ¶ 2-201.12(A)(1) if the employee is asymptomatic for at least 24 hours or provides written medical documentation from a health practitioner stating the symptom is from a noninfectious condition (¶ 2-201.13(A)(1)); Table 1a of Annex 3 confirms "Vomiting – EXCLUDE 2-201.12(A)(1)" in both HSP and non-HSP facilities, with no regulatory authority approval needed if the employee is not diagnosed. [food-code-2026:p61:c0; food-code-2026:p388:c0]
- The handbook can still be used as a practical training and implementation aid: it presents employee health requirements in question-and-answer format with forms, tables, and decision trees for management and employees, and its Table 1a for vomiting matches the 2026 Code (exclude under 2-201.12(A)(1); return when asymptomatic for at least 24 hours or with medical documentation; no RA approval if not diagnosed). [employee-health-handbook:p3:c0; employee-health-handbook:p31:c0; food-code-2026:p388:c0]
- Its operational direction is likewise usable where consistent with the 2026 Code, e.g. the manager's response to a reported vomiting or diarrhea symptom: ask the employee to stop work immediately and leave the food establishment, with return to work no sooner than 24 hours after vomiting and diarrhea have ended. [employee-health-handbook:p7:c2; food-code-2026:p61:c0]
- Because the handbook's section citations trace to the 2017 edition, section numbers and any details should be verified against the 2026 Food Code, which controls on divergence; for example, the 2026 Code requires a food establishment to have written procedures for responding to vomiting or diarrheal events under ¶ 2-502.11 (and the employee health policy/exclusion-reinstatement provisions at §§ 2-201.11, 2-201.12, 2-201.13). [employee-health-handbook:p3:c0; food-code-2026:p72:c0; food-code-2026:p684:c2]
