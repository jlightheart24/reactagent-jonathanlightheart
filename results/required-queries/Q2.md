## Final result

- Employee's immediate actions (per the handbook, if at work): stop work immediately, report to management, and go home — food employees and conditional employees shall report symptoms such as vomiting immediately to the person in charge. [employee-health-handbook:p6:c0; employee-health-handbook:p31:c0]
- Manager's immediate action: ask the food employee to stop work immediately and leave the food establishment; the person in charge (PIC) has the authority to exclude the employee, and "exclusion" means the employee is not permitted to work in or enter the food establishment (areas where food is received, prepared, stored, packaged, served, vended, transported, or purchased). [employee-health-handbook:p7:c2; employee-health-handbook:p10:c0]
- Under the handbook's Table 1a (2-201.12), vomiting requires EXCLUSION both in facilities serving a highly susceptible population and in facilities not serving an HSP, under 2-201.12(A)(1). [employee-health-handbook:p31:c0]
- Earliest return to work: no sooner than 24 hours after vomiting (and diarrhea) have ended — Table 1a removes the exclusion when the excluded food employee has been asymptomatic for at least 24 hours, or provides medical documentation (2-201.13(A)(1)); regulatory authority approval is not required if the employee is not diagnosed. [employee-health-handbook:p7:c2; employee-health-handbook:p6:c0; employee-health-handbook:p31:c0]
- Exception if diagnosed: if the vomiting is linked to a diagnosis of Norovirus, Shigella spp., STEC, hepatitis A virus, or typhoid fever, the Table 1a 24-hour rule does not apply — for example, with Norovirus the employee works only on a restricted basis 24 hours after symptoms resolve in a non-HSP facility (remains excluded in an HSP facility), and the restriction/exclusion continues until regulatory authority approval plus medical clearance or more than 48 hours since becoming asymptomatic. [employee-health-handbook:p31:c0; employee-health-handbook:p33:c1]
- The manager should also discard ready-to-eat food that may have been contaminated by an employee who has been restricted or excluded as specified in Section 2-201.12. [employee-health-handbook:p10:c2]
- Because a vomiting event in the establishment can spread pathogens, the handbook points to written procedures under Section 2-501.11 for cleaning and disinfecting vomitus/fecal contamination, covering containment and removal of discharges (including airborne particulates), cleaning/sanitizing/disinfecting contaminated surfaces, evaluating and disposing of exposed food, use of EPA-registered disinfectants sufficient to inactivate norovirus and personal protective equipment, segregation of contaminated areas, and minimizing transmission by excluding or restricting ill employees under 2-201.12. [employee-health-handbook:p20:c0; employee-health-handbook:p20:c1]
- Source status: the handbook is FDA operational guidance published in 2020 based on the earlier 2017 Food Code, not current model-code text; the current 2026 FDA Food Code (a model code, binding only where adopted) carries the same rule in Table 1a — exclude for vomiting and reinstate when asymptomatic for at least 24 hours or with written medical documentation from a health practitioner that the symptom is from a noninfectious condition (2-201.13(A)(1)). [employee-health-handbook:p31:c0; food-code-2026:p388:c0; food-code-2026:p61:c0]
