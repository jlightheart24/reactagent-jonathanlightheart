**Question (as run):** According to FDA Food Code 2026 sections 2-201.12(A)(1) and 2-201.13(A)(1), what must the person in charge do when a food employee has vomiting or diarrhea from no known noninfectious condition, and when may that employee be reinstated?

## Final result

- Under FDA Food Code 2026 § 2-201.12, the PERSON IN CHARGE shall EXCLUDE or RESTRICT a FOOD EMPLOYEE from a FOOD ESTABLISHMENT, and under ¶ 2-201.12(A)(1), except when the symptom is from a noninfectious condition, the person in charge must EXCLUDE a food employee who is symptomatic with vomiting or diarrhea. [food-code-2026:p59:c0]
- Under § 2-201.13 (Removal, Adjustment, or Retention of Exclusions and Restrictions), ¶ 2-201.13(A)(1) provides that a food employee EXCLUDED as specified under Subparagraph 2-201.12(A)(1) may be reinstated if the employee (a) is ASYMPTOMATIC for at least 24 hours, or (b) provides to the PERSON IN CHARGE written medical documentation from a HEALTH PRACTITIONER stating that the symptom is from a noninfectious condition; this applies except when the employee is diagnosed with typhoid fever or an infection from hepatitis A virus. [food-code-2026:p61:c0]
- Annex 3 Table 1a summarizes that for vomiting or diarrhea the employee is EXCLUDED under 2-201.12(A)(1) in both HSP-serving and non-HSP facilities, and is removed from exclusion when asymptomatic for at least 24 hours or upon providing medical documentation under 2-201.13(A)(1), with no regulatory authority approval needed if the employee is not diagnosed with a listed pathogen. [food-code-2026:p388:c0]
- An excluded individual may not work as a food employee on the premises of any food establishment, and the person in charge may allow undiagnosed food employees who were initially symptomatic and whose symptoms have resolved to return to work in a full capacity 24 hours after symptoms resolve (Annex 3 public health reason guidance for 2-201.13). [food-code-2026:p404:c0]
- If the vomiting/diarrhea employee is instead diagnosed with a listed pathogen (e.g., norovirus under 2-201.12(A)(2)), the simpler 24-hour reinstatement no longer suffices: reinstatement under ¶ 2-201.13(D) requires APPROVAL from the REGULATORY AUTHORITY plus either written medical documentation from a HEALTH PRACTITIONER that the employee is free of norovirus infection, or more than 48 hours having passed since the employee became ASYMPTOMATIC after symptoms of vomiting or diarrhea resolved. [food-code-2026:p391:c1; food-code-2026:p63:c1]
- These provisions are from the FDA Food Code 2026, a model code that is legally binding only where adopted by a jurisdiction; the section text is marked "P" (provision addressed by a core or noncore item identified in the Food Code). [food-code-2026:p59:c0]
