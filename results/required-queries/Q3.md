## Final result

- Infective dose: the Bad Bug Book (2012 FDA pathogen reference, not a regulation) states the norovirus infective dose is very low, estimated to be as low as 1 to 10 viral particles, while viral particles are excreted at high levels by both symptomatic and asymptomatic people (as high as 1 x 10^12 viral particles per gram of feces). [bad-bug-book:p169:c0]
- Onset: a mild, brief illness usually develops between 24 and 48 hours after consuming contaminated food or water (median in outbreaks 33 to 36 hours), with onset times within 12 hours of exposure reported; the consumer snapshot likewise says symptoms usually start within 1 or 2 days but may start in as few as 12 hours. [bad-bug-book:p169:c0; bad-bug-book:p168:c2]
- Characteristic symptoms: acute-onset vomiting (often explosive), watery non-bloody diarrhea with abdominal cramps, and nausea; explosive, projectile vomiting is usually the first sign of illness and is often used to characterize the illness, and headache, low-grade fever, chills, and muscle aches may also occur. [bad-bug-book:p169:c1]
- Course of illness: symptoms generally persist for 12 to 60 hours (mean 24 to 48 hours), with most people feeling better within 1 to 2 days; dehydration is the most common complication, especially among the very young, the elderly, and those with underlying medical conditions. [bad-bug-book:p169:c2]
- Food-worker contamination of ready-to-eat food: foodborne norovirus illnesses are epidemiologically linked to consumption of ready-to-eat (RTE) foods contaminated by food workers (along with environmental contamination of produce and contaminated molluscan shellfish), with transmission occurring through the fecal-oral route (or vomit on occasion) and often associated with improper sanitation controls or their application. [bad-bug-book:p170:c0; bad-bug-book:p170:c1]
- Any ready-to-eat food handled by ill food workers may be contaminated; salad ingredients, fruit, and oysters are the foods most often implicated, and nearly 29% of all norovirus foodborne outbreaks from 1997-2004 were attributed to food purchased or served at a restaurant or delicatessen. [bad-bug-book:p170:c1]
- Contamination can occur without visible illness: about 30% of infected people show no gastrointestinal illness or symptoms yet still excrete high levels of virus in stool ("silent shedders"), and the virus may continue to pass in bowel movements even after symptoms resolve; alcohol-based antibacterial hand gels do not protect against norovirus, so handwashing is especially important. [bad-bug-book:p169:c2; bad-bug-book:p168:c3]
